Common questions

Who's the example family?

Two adults and two children (the youngest is at primary school), both parents working. They tumble dry 5 loads a week in a condenser dryer, cook 4 dinners a week in an electric oven, have an electric shower, two phones on EE past their contract at £55 a month, BT broadband past its contract, and a £125 weekly shop at Tesco without a Clubcard. The free changes, money-saving buys, bills and food shop add up to £1,798 a year. That's before any money they might be owed. Your total depends on your answers.

Is it really free?

Yes. There's no sign-up, no email and nothing to pay. If you buy something through one of our Amazon links, Amazon may pay us a small commission. It never costs you more, and it never changes what we suggest. That's why we'll tell you when something isn't worth buying.

What happens to my answers?

They stay on your phone. We never see them, and there's no account to make. To see how many people visit, we count page views with Cloudflare Web Analytics. It uses no cookies and doesn't identify you.

Where do the numbers come from?

Which? appliance tests, the Energy Saving Trust, Nesta, Ofgem's price cap and GOV.UK. Every figure is listed under "How we worked it out" in your results.

Why do you tell me not to buy things?

Because a gadget that costs more to run than what you've got isn't a saving. On big loads, a heated airer can cost more than a tumble dryer, so we say so.
